# BurnRate Runbook

Alerts are generated by sloth from `slo/burnrate.sloth.yaml`. Each SLO has two
alerts with the same name and a `severity` label:

| Severity | Fires when | Budget spent if it continues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `page` | 5m and 1h burn rate both above 14.4x, or 30m and 6h both above 6x | 2% in 1h, or 5% in 6h |
| `ticket` | 2h and 1d burn rate both above 3x, or 6h and 3d both above 1x | 10% in 1d, or 10% in 3d |

Open the **BurnRate / SLOs** Grafana dashboard first: it shows the SLI, the
remaining budget and the burn rate per window for both SLOs.

## Availability

`BurnRateCheckAvailability`: too many `/v1/check` responses are 5xx.

1. Check whether a rollout is in progress:
   `kubectl -n burnrate get rollout burnrate` (or `kubectl argo rollouts get rollout burnrate -n burnrate`).
   A canary with a failing AnalysisRun aborts on its own; if it did not, abort it:
   `kubectl argo rollouts abort burnrate -n burnrate`.
2. Look at the error mix on the dashboard ("Requests per second by status").
   `500` with `"error":"injected fault"` in the body means `FAULT_ERROR_RATE`
   is set; reset it to `0` in the Helm values.
3. Check pod health: `kubectl -n burnrate get pods`, restarts, OOM kills
   (`kubectl -n burnrate describe pod <pod>`), and logs:
   `kubectl -n burnrate logs -l app.kubernetes.io/name=burnrate --tail=100`.
   Every log line has a `trace_id` to look up in the trace backend.
4. If pods are CPU-throttled, check the HPA is not pinned at `maxReplicas`:
   `kubectl -n burnrate get hpa burnrate`.

## Latency

`BurnRateCheckLatency`: more than 1% of `/v1/check` calls take over 100 ms.

1. Compare p50, p95 and p99 on the dashboard. A uniform shift points at CPU
   throttling or `FAULT_LATENCY`; only the tail moving points at GC or lock
   contention on hot keys.
2. Check CPU per pod against the 500m limit and whether the HPA is scaling.
3. Check `burnrate_active_buckets`: a sudden jump means a client is sending
   high-cardinality keys. Lower `BUCKET_IDLE_TTL` or rate-limit that tenant.
4. Roll back the last change if latency moved with a release:
   `kubectl argo rollouts undo burnrate -n burnrate`.
